# FDA user guide

Put your own PDF, Word, Excel, CSV, Markdown or text files in this folder (fda/docs) and ask FDA about them,
for example "what does the billing policy say about refunds?". FDA answers from the files and names the file
and the page or section. Nothing leaves the device.

## Talking to FDA
Just talk: FDA listens while it speaks. To stop it, say "stop" or "wait". To let it finish what it was saying,
say "continue" or "go on": it picks up at the sentence where it was cut off. Short sounds like "uh huh" or
"mm-hmm" do not interrupt it. FDA ignores the TV, the radio and people talking to each other.

## Building apps and games
Say "make me a snake game", "build a tip calculator" or "make a page for my coffee shop". A progress bar shows
while it builds (about ten seconds for a game). The app appears on the display when it is ready.

## Changing a game while you play
Say "change the bird to a butterfly", "make the pipes a photo of bamboo", "make the sky dark blue" or
"make it faster". These change at once, without rebuilding the game.
For new features say "add a high score" or "add a second level": FDA rewrites the game and tests it.

## Going back to an earlier version
Say "undo that" to go back one change, or "go back two versions". Every build, change and tweak is saved.

## Checking the device
Say "check this device" or "is the Pi secure?". FDA checks open network services, waiting updates, SSH
settings, files anyone can change, disk space, memory, temperature and power. It only reads; it never changes
anything. The full report is saved in the reports folder.

## Everyday help
Weather ("what's the weather tomorrow?"), news headlines, recipes, web search for current facts, the time,
calculations, timers, alarms, reminders, lights, temperature and music.
